# The Play listing

ADDENDUM-03 Part 19: "The store listing leads with what the person will be able to do.
The first screenshot is the session mid set."

Everything below is final copy. The banned list in DESIGN.md section 6 applies to every
word of it, and so does the register from the addendum's WHO THIS IS FOR section:
nothing implying the ceiling is holding steady, nothing implying improvement is
unlikely, nothing congratulating anybody for not getting worse, nothing treating
maintenance as second best.

## App name

Steady Health

## Short description, 80 characters

    What can you do? Get stronger, at home, in a few minutes a day.

79 characters. It leads with the question the whole app is positioned on, and the
promise after it is about what the person will be able to do rather than about the
app.

## Full description

**What can you do?**

Not how many steps you took. What you can actually do: get up off the floor, carry the
shopping in one trip, take the stairs without stopping, stand on one foot while you put
your trousers on.

Steady Health keeps track of those four things and helps you do more of them.

**A few minutes, led out loud**

A session is four to eight minutes. The app names the movement, tells you how to set
up, counts you in, and counts your repetitions out loud, so you can put the phone on a
table and move rather than read a screen. Everything gets a little harder only when you
say it felt easy.

There is a way to do every session sitting down, and a way to do one in bed. You pick
how you get around and the app is a different app.

**The programme your therapist gave you**

Photograph the sheet from your physiotherapist or occupational therapist and the app
reads it, shows you every line, and asks you to check it before anything is saved. Then
it runs that programme as they set it. It does not add movements, take them away, or
change the numbers. At the end it makes a one page summary to bring to your next
appointment: what you were given, what you did, when, and what hurt.

**Once a month, ten minutes**

A short check with a chair and a wall tells you what you can do now against what you
could do before. Staying the same counts, and the app says so, because strength drops
on its own from midlife if nothing is done about it.

**Nothing leaves your phone**

No account. No sign up. No email address. No advertising, no tracking, and no analytics
beyond what Android does on its own. Everything you type, photograph or measure stays
on this phone, encrypted, and there is no copy anywhere else because there is nowhere
else. You can export everything as ordinary spreadsheets whenever you like, and you can
delete all of it in two taps.

**Free**

All of it. There is nothing to buy and nothing held back.

**Not a medical device**

Steady Health keeps a record and leads a session. It does not diagnose anything, it
does not tell you what your numbers mean for you, and it never advises for or against
anything a clinician told you. Those are questions for whoever is looking after you.

## What is deliberately not in the listing

- No age, no "senior", no "older adults", nowhere. The app never names an age and
  neither does the listing.
- No falling, no risk, no prevention. That framing sells to worried adult children
  rather than to the person who will use it, and it is not the app's register.
- No before-and-after, no numbers of users, no testimonials, no clinical claims.
- No mention of AI in the short description or the first screen of the long one. The
  optional on-device model is one paragraph deep in the app's own settings, and leading
  with it would promise something most people never turn on.

## Blocked on the owner

Play Console entry, the content rating questionnaire, and the data safety form. The
data safety answers are all "no data collected", which is the whole form, but it has to
be filled in by the account holder.

# Research 3: Evidence Base for Exercise Profiling and Movement Progression

Produced September 2026 for Steady. Everything here is meant to be encoded as rules, thresholds, tables, and content. Steady is not a medical device; it does not diagnose or prescribe; all user-facing framing stays general wellness and education.

## TL;DR
- Do not embed the PAR-Q+ in the app. The PAR-Q+ Collaboration's terms state the form "must remain unaltered and may not be incorporated into electronic surveys, research tools, or any derivative materials without the express written consent of the PAR-Q+ Collaboration." Build an original, movement-framed self-check and link out to eparmedx.com for the real instrument. Screen loosely; over-referral is a proven barrier to participation.
- The neutral movement-exclusion architecture maps cleanly onto real contraindications: breath-holding and Valsalva, spinal flexion plus rotation, overhead pressing, floor transfers, impact, deep knee bend. The single most important reversal to hard-code: graded or incremental exercise is contraindicated in ME/CFS and post-exertional-malaise long COVID (NICE NG206, 2021). That ladder must be replaced by pacing within an energy envelope and must never auto-progress.
- Default to the talk test for intensity, per-session spike caps instead of the discredited 10% rule, and general-wellness language throughout. Every persona needs a defined start point, explicitly hidden ladders, and a substitution rule so nobody ends up with an empty programme.

## Part 1. Screening instruments
PAR-Q+ (2024 version, copyright PAR-Q+ Collaboration; Warburton, Gledhill, Jamnik, McKenzie). Page 1: seven questions (heart condition or high blood pressure per a doctor; chest pain at rest or with activity; loss of balance from dizziness or loss of consciousness in 12 months; other chronic condition; prescribed medication for a chronic condition; bone, joint or soft-tissue problem that could worsen; told to only exercise under supervision). All no: cleared, start with 20 to 60 minutes low-to-moderate activity 3 to 5 days a week building to 150 minutes. Any yes: pages 2 and 3 cover ten condition clusters; any yes there routes to ePARmed-X+ online or a qualified exercise professional. Clearance valid 12 months maximum.

Licensing: "You must use the entire questionnaire and NO changes are permitted"; it may not be incorporated into electronic surveys or derivative materials without written consent. Steady cannot re-implement it in-app. Options: request written consent, or build an original movement-framed self-check (recommended) and link to the official instrument.

ACSM 2015 preparticipation algorithm (Riebe et al., MSSE 2015): three inputs (regular activity in the last three months; known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease or symptoms; desired intensity); outputs "start or progress" versus "seek clearance." Designed to reduce unnecessary referrals. Aggressive screening deters the sedentary people who benefit most; exercise-related cardiac events are usually preceded by warning symptoms; risk falls as fitness rises.

Self-report screening trades sensitivity for reach. Honest framing: a self-check reduces obvious risk and prompts a conversation; it does not certify safety.

Recommendation: a short movement-framed self-check with a few "talk to a clinician first" flags in original wording (chest pain; fainting or dizziness; told to only exercise under supervision; pregnancy; a new or worsening bone or joint problem) plus the movement-exclusion questions. Present clearance as "start slowly and build up," never as medical clearance.

## Part 2. Contraindications mapped to neutral exclusion tags
Hernia (abdominal, umbilical, inguinal, hiatal), post-repair, post-abdominal surgery, post-caesarean. Mechanism: intra-abdominal pressure spikes from Valsalva. Avoid heavy lifting, breath-holding, forceful abdominal work, high impact, heavy bracing. Safe: walking, cycling, swimming, breath-controlled light resistance. Tags: straining or holding my breath; anything that strains my stomach; getting down on the floor (early recovery).

Pregnancy and postpartum (ACOG Committee Opinion 804, 2020): activity is safe and desirable absent contraindications; 150 minutes a week moderate plus strengthening including pelvic floor. Avoid motionless standing and supine positions in trimesters 2 and 3, fall and trauma risk. Warning signs mean stop and consult. Diastasis recti, pelvic floor dysfunction, prolapse: avoid loaded spinal flexion and high intra-abdominal pressure. Tags: lying flat on my back; anything that strains my stomach; jumping or impact.

Hypertension and Valsalva: intra-arterial recordings during breath-hold leg press peaked at a group mean of 320/250 mmHg (MacDougall 1985); open-glottis exhalation drops this toward 198/175. PAR-Q+ flags resting BP 160/90 or more. Tags: straining or holding my breath; de-emphasise maximal pressing.

Osteoporosis and osteopenia (Royal Osteoporosis Society; Osteoporosis Canada; IOF): avoid loaded spinal flexion, flexion plus rotation (a twist adds about 40% spinal load), high impact, loading overhead. Encourage back-extension strengthening, weight-bearing, resistance, balance. Tags: deep forward bending or curling my spine; twisting my back; jumping or impact; lifting overhead.

Knee and hip osteoarthritis; joint replacement: low-impact strengthening reduces pain; combined diet and exercise lowers knee loads (IDEA RCT). Posterior-approach hip precautions: no hip flexion past 90 degrees, no adduction past midline, no internal rotation early. Surgeon-dependent; keep as user-declared limits. Tags: deep knee bending; getting down on the floor (early post-op).

Low back pain, disc issues, spinal stenosis: osteoporosis avoids flexion; stenosis often prefers flexion and avoids extension. Offer both flexion- and extension-biased gentle options; keep what feels good. Tags: deep forward bending; arching my back; twisting my back.

Shoulder impingement and rotator cuff: keep pressing below shoulder height. Tag: lifting overhead.

Obesity class II and III: start with chair, water, and cycling for those with about 100 lb or more excess weight (Obesity Medicine Association). Address joint torque, heat load, breathlessness, floor transfers, chair egress. Seated exercise improves strength when standing tolerance is limited. Resistance training preserves muscle. Show seated and standing-with-support first; hide floor work until a floor-transfer sub-skill is achieved; rest and hydration prompts; short bouts.

Type 2 diabetes (ADA Standards of Care 2023 to 2026): 150 minutes a week over at least 3 days, no more than 2 consecutive rest days, plus 2 to 3 resistance sessions. Neuropathy: foot inspection, footwear, favour non-weight-bearing if significant. Retinopathy: avoid vigorous and Valsalva work. Hypoglycaemia risk with insulin or sulfonylureas: plain-language note to have fast carbohydrate available and check with a clinician about timing; never dosing. Tags: straining or holding my breath; jumping or impact; foot-care reminder flag.

Cardiovascular disease and heart failure: PAR-Q+ flags CAD with no activity in two months, heart failure, arrhythmias. Cleared patients benefit from moderate aerobic and light resistance with exhale on effort. Require self-reported clinician clearance before any progressing ladder; talk-test-capped moderate; hide vigorous intervals.

COPD and asthma: exercise is beneficial; dyspnoea-limited pacing, intervals, Borg dyspnoea scale; current asthma symptoms route to clinician first.

Balance impairment and fall risk: route to the balance ladder plus lower-body strengthening.

Peripheral artery disease: walk to moderate claudication pain, rest, repeat (ACC 2025); encode interval walk-to-discomfort rather than continuous distance.

Lymphoedema: progressive resistance training does not worsen and can improve it; start light, progress slowly, monitor the limb. Not an exclusion.

ME/CFS and long COVID with post-exertional malaise: NICE NG206 (October 2021) states graded exercise therapy should not be offered; PEM is a core symptom; remain within the energy limit and do not push through symptoms. CDC warns standard recommendations can be harmful. App logic: if the user reports a PEM pattern (markedly worse for days after exertion), switch to non-progressing pacing mode with a user-set envelope, no automatic increases, symptom-triggered de-escalation, and explicit "this is not graded exercise" framing.

Pelvic organ prolapse: avoid heavy intra-abdominal pressure and impact. Retinal detachment risk and eye surgery: avoid Valsalva, impact, head-down positions. Also: temporary illness or fever (defer), recent concussion (clinician), uncontrolled seizures.

## Part 3. Progression science
Graded activity increases a quota on a time-contingent schedule; graded exposure targets fear-avoidance. Neither for PEM.

Aerobic: 20 to 60 minutes low-to-moderate, 3 to 5 days, building to 150 minutes a week; duration before frequency before intensity. Resistance (ACSM): novices 2 to 3 days a week; single sets effective for older and novice; 8 to 12 reps (10 to 15 older); progress load 2 to 10% when 1 to 2 reps beyond target are possible; 48 hours rest per muscle group.

The 10% rule has weak evidence. Frandsen et al. (BJSM 2025; 5,205 runners, 1,820 injuries, 588,071 sessions): the predictor is single-session distance spike relative to the longest run in the prior 30 days. Versus at most 10% increase: small spikes HRR 1.64, moderate (over 30 to 100%) HRR 1.52, large (over 100%) HRR 1.74. ACWR (Gabbett 2016; sweet spot 0.8 to 1.3, elevated over 1.5) is contested. For Steady: per-session spike cap of about 110 to 120% of the largest recent same-type session; ACWR optional.

Intensity: talk test (validated proxy for first ventilatory threshold; moderate = can talk but not sing; vigorous = only a few words). Borg RPE 12 to 14 or CR10 3 to 4 moderate.

Older adults: single-set resistance, chair and standing-with-support, balance, power. Obesity: seated, water, cycling, short bouts. Returners: restart below the last level.

Re-entry defaults (precautionary): one week off, resume; two weeks, 90 to 100% with one easy session; one month, 70 to 80% rebuilding over 1 to 2 weeks; three months, 50 to 60% over 3 to 4 weeks; six months, 40 to 50% over 4 to 6 weeks; one year, beginner.

Minimum effective dose: Rodriguez et al. (BJSM 2025; 11 RCTs, n=414): bouts of at most 5 minutes, at least twice a day, 3 or more days a week, weekly totals of 4.5 to 67.5 minutes, produced large fitness gains (g about 1.37, moderate certainty), 91.1% compliance, 82.8% adherence. Stamatakis et al. (Nature Medicine 2022; 25,241 non-exercisers): median 4.4 minutes a day of vigorous intermittent activity associated with 26 to 30% lower all-cause mortality and 32 to 34% lower CVD mortality. "Every move counts" is evidence-based.

Beginner resistance: sit-to-stand, wall push-ups, step-ups, heel raises, seated band rows; one set of 8 to 12; exhale on effort; add reps, then a second set, then light load.

## Part 4. Exercise menu and ladders
Walking and cardio: seated marching, standing marching with support, short flat indoor walk (2 to 5 minutes), continuous flat walk building duration, add pace (talk test), add gentle incline, interval walking. Claudication: walk to discomfort, rest, repeat.

Chair-based: seated marching; arm circles and raises (below shoulder if overhead excluded); leg extensions; toe and heel taps; band chest press and rows; seated reach cardio; seated upright core.

Standing strength: wall push-ups to counter to incline; sit-to-stand from high surface, standard chair, lower surface, no hands; step-ups low to higher; heel raises with support to single leg; mini squats to chair. Control the lowering; exhale on effort.

Floor work and floor rise (a distinct trainable skill that matters for independence): supported kneeling transfers, half-kneel to stand with chair, floor-to-stand via side-sit, unassisted; then glute bridges, bird-dog, extension. Hidden when "getting down on the floor" is excluded.

Balance (mirrors CDC four-stage): feet together, semi-tandem, tandem, single leg, single leg with head turns or eyes closed. Anchor to Otago (17 strength and balance exercises plus walking, 3 times a week; about 35 to 40% fewer falls in frail older adults), Tai Chi (31 to 58% reductions in reviews), LiFE. Always a wall or sturdy chair within reach.

Pushing: wall, high incline, low incline, knees, full floor. Hidden when pushing or floor is excluded. Below shoulder if overhead excluded.

Flexibility: static stretching improves range but does not prevent injury or reduce soreness meaningfully; offer as comfort and warm-up, not as an injury-prevention claim.

Water: strongly supported for joint pain and high body weight; offer as an alternative ladder where access exists.

Substitution rule: pushing to seated band press in range or lower body; floor to standing or seated core and chair transfers; impact to walking, cycling, water; overhead to raises below shoulder; deep knee bend to partial-range sit-to-stand or step-ups. Guaranteed floor when nearly everything is excluded: seated ladder plus talk-test walking snacks plus breath-controlled band work.

## Part 5. Tests and norms
Safety rules: sturdy chair or wall within reach; spotter advised for single-leg, four-stage, 6MWT and Cooper in higher-risk users; absolute contraindication to strength or balance testing is recent lower-extremity fracture or post-op weight-bearing restriction; no maximal walk or run tests with unstable angina or MI within a month. Present results as wellness feedback, never diagnosis.

30-second chair stand (Rikli and Jones): 17-inch chair against wall, arms crossed, count full stands. Below-average lower bounds. Men: 60 to 64 under 14; 65 to 69 under 12; 70 to 74 under 12; 75 to 79 under 11; 80 to 84 under 10; 85 to 89 under 8; 90 to 94 under 7. Women: 60 to 64 under 12; 65 to 69 under 11; 70 to 74 under 10; 75 to 79 under 10; 80 to 84 under 9; 85 to 89 under 8; 90 to 94 under 4. MDC 2 to 3 reps; 4 or more meaningful. Healthy 19 to 35 average about 33.

Five times sit-to-stand: 43 to 45 cm chair, arms folded. Over 15 s is 74% greater recurrent-fall risk (Buatois 2008); 12 s or more is a screening flag. Means: 20s about 6 s; 40s 7.6; 60 to 69 7.8; 70 to 79 9.3; 80 to 85 10.8.

Timed up and go: armchair, walk 3 m, turn, return, sit. 12 s or more CDC STEADI fall-risk threshold; 13.5 s or more high risk (Makizako 2017 about 90% correct classification).

Gait speed: time the middle 6 m of a 10 m walk. Under 0.8 m/s slow (EWGSOP2); 1.0 or more normal; under 0.6 significant impairment; MCID 0.1 m/s.

Single-leg stance: hands on hips, best of three, age-dependent. Eyes-open means: 18 to 39 about 43 s; 40 to 49 about 40; 50 to 59 about 37; 60 to 69 about 27; 70 to 79 about 15; 80 plus about 5.6. Eyes closed drops sharply (60 to 69 about 2 s). Under 10 s eyes open flags elevated risk. Verify Springer per-cell values against the primary paper (longer cap used) before hard-coding.

Four-stage balance (CDC STEADI): feet side-by-side, semi-tandem, tandem, one foot, up to 10 s each, advance only on success. Cannot hold tandem 10 s means increased fall risk; route to the balance ladder.

Six-minute walk (ATS 2002): 30 m corridor, walk as far as possible in 6 minutes. Contraindicated with unstable angina or MI within a month. Enright and Sherrill 1998: men 6MWD = 7.57 x height cm minus 5.02 x age minus 1.76 x weight kg minus 309; women = 2.11 x height minus 2.29 x weight minus 5.78 x age plus 667. Lower limit of normal is predicted minus 153 m (men) or 139 m (women). Phone-only: GPS on a known flat route with caveats.

Two-minute step test (Rikli and Jones): march raising each knee to midpoint between patella and iliac crest; count right-knee raises in 2 minutes. Examples: women 60 to 64 25th to 75th percentile 75 to 107; men 87 to 115; women 90 to 94 44 to 72; men 52 to 86. Under 65 associated with lower functional ability. Floor-free cardio proxy.

Push-up, plank, wall sit: for fitter personas; improvement-over-self primary. Contraindicate push-up test with shoulder or wrist exclusions or floor exclusion; wall sit with deep knee bend exclusion.

Cooper 12-minute (Cooper 1968): maximal; recreational persona only. VO2max = (distance m minus 504.9) / 44.73. Verify category bands against a Cooper Institute source; the formula is stable.

Population fit: chair stand, 5xSTS, TUG, four-stage, gait speed, two-minute step for older, fall risk, obesity, deconditioned. 6MWT for moderate fitness. Push-up, plank, wall sit, Cooper for recreational, cleared, no CVD flags.

## Part 6. Persona configurations (show, hide, start, progress)
1. Sedentary, class II to III obesity: show seated, standing-with-support, walking snacks, water; hide floor (until transfer skill), impact, deep knee bend; start 2 to 5 minutes seated plus short walks, one set of 10 to 15; add duration and reps first, hydration prompts, talk-test moderate.
2. Sedentary but healthy: show all; start brisk walking toward 150 minutes plus beginner full-body; duration, frequency, then intensity; spike cap.
3. Older adult with fall risk: show balance (Otago-style), lower-body strength, walking; hide impact; advance balance stance only after a 10 s hold; retest monthly.
4. Joint replacement: seated and standing in cleared range, walking; hide deep knee bend, deep hip flexion and floor early, impact; surgeon-guided; TUG and 6MWT to track.
5. Abdominal wall issue: walking, cycling, breath-controlled bands, lower body; hide straining, loaded abdominal flexion, impact, heavy bracing; reps before load; stop on bulge or pain.
6. CVD cleared: moderate aerobic, light resistance; hide vigorous intervals and straining; require self-reported clearance gate.
7. Type 2 diabetes: aerobic plus resistance with foot-care reminder; hide vigorous or Valsalva if retinopathy; non-weight-bearing if significant neuropathy; hypoglycaemia note.
8. Postpartum: pelvic floor, gradual core, walking, low impact; hide loaded flexion, high impact, heavy intra-abdominal pressure early; defer to clinician on timeline.
9. Chronic pain without PEM: graded activity and exposure options; hide user-selected painful patterns; start below flare threshold; gentle time-contingent quota.
10. ME/CFS or long COVID with PEM: pacing mode only; hide all progressing ladders; disable auto-progression; never auto-increase; symptom-triggered de-escalation; explicit "this is not graded exercise" copy.
11. Recreationally active: all ladders plus push-up, plank, wall sit, Cooper; start at current level; spike-capped periodised increases.
12. On a GLP-1: resistance training as priority (2 to 3 times a week, progressive) plus neutral protein reminder; add reps, sets, then load.

## Part 7. Legal and safety framing
FDA General Wellness policy (2019): exempt if the product maintains or encourages general health or associates a healthy lifestyle with reduced chronic-condition risk, makes no claim to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease, and is low risk. Avoid outcome claims ("lowers your blood pressure," "treats diabetes," "prevents falls"); prefer "supports general fitness," "many people find," "general activity guidelines suggest."

FTC: any specific health-outcome claim in marketing needs competent and reliable scientific evidence. The Health Breach Notification Rule reaches health apps; local-first with no backend is a point in favour.

Enforcement: no prominent US case establishes liability for a general fitness app over exercise injury; enforcement centres on deceptive claims and privacy. The practical risk is claims, not injury torts.

Load-bearing disclaimers: not a medical device, does not diagnose or treat; consult a clinician before starting if you have a condition or symptoms; stop and seek help for chest pain, faintness, severe breathlessness; symptom-based stop rules inside each session; honest data-handling statement. Decorative: "results may vary," long EULAs. A contextual in-flow safety prompt beats a buried disclaimer.

PAR-Q+: link out with attribution to the PAR-Q+ Collaboration (Warburton, Jamnik, Bredin and Gledhill, Health and Fitness Journal of Canada 4(2):3-23, 2011); do not reproduce or adapt in-app without written consent.

Open-source fitness apps mostly avoid the problem by not giving personalised guidance. Steady does more tailoring and therefore must be more careful with language.

## Caveats
PAR-Q+ licensing is the hardest constraint and is stated by the Collaboration itself; a one-time lawyer review of store listing and disclaimers is prudent. Detraining percentages and the spike-cap number are precautionary engineering defaults; label as tunable. Verify Springer single-leg per-cell values and Cooper category bands before hard-coding. The PEM reversal is guideline-established but contested by some clinicians; encode the cautious position because the downside is severe. Retinopathy, joint-replacement, and postpartum precautions are clinician-dependent; treat user-declared limits as authoritative and never infer or override them.
